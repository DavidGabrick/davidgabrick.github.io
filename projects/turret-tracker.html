Autonomous Turret Tracker

A 2-axis pan-tilt vision system that detects and tracks moving targets in real time.

Overview

The Autonomous Turret Tracker is a 2-axis pan-tilt platform that uses computer vision to detect and follow a target, such as a human face or a moving object like a drone, and physically aim at it in real time. It uses a 3D-printed mechanical assembly, an on-board microcontroller, and a vision pipeline running on a host computer.

The Objective

Build a low-cost system that can locate a target within a camera frame and keep it centered by driving two servo axes, with control commands sent wirelessly to the turret rather than over a wired tether. The project has four parts that need to work together: the mechanical pan-tilt assembly, the vision pipeline, the control law that turns image error into motion, and the firmware and power electronics that drive the servos.

Approach & Process

Mechanical design

The turret uses an MG996R servo on the pan axis and an SG90 servo on the tilt axis, mounted on a custom bracket modeled in Fusion 360 and 3D-printed in PLA. The two-axis arrangement gives the camera independent horizontal and vertical aim.

Computer-vision pipeline

A Python pipeline built on OpenCV processes a USB webcam feed and performs real-time detection of human faces and moving objects such as drones. For each detection it computes the centroid error (how far the target is from the center of the frame) on both axes.

Control law

A proportional (P) controller maps the pixel error on each axis to a servo-angle correction. Those joint-angle targets are what get sent to the turret, closing the loop so the camera continuously re-centers on the target.

Firmware & wireless link

The joint-angle targets are transmitted wirelessly to an ESP32 over Wi-Fi. Firmware written in C++ on the Arduino framework, using the ESP32Servo library, receives the targets and drives both axes via PWM. To suppress noise coming back onto the ESP32 rail from the servos, the power lines are conditioned with 100 µF decoupling capacitors and flyback diodes.
